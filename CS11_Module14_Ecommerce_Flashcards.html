Expert analysis: At $2,000 a month, Basic looks cheapest ($69 vs. $70 for Pro), but Basic allows only 100 SKUs and Valley Threads has 140, so Basic isn't actually an option. Pro is the best choice. It's the cheapest plan that fits today ($70), and at $10,000 a month it stays the cheapest ($150 vs. $179 for Unlimited and $199 for Business). The key relationship: a lower percentage fee matters more as sales grow, so a slightly higher monthly fee can pay for itself. Unlimited (0% fee) would only beat Pro once sales pass $12,900 a month, where $50 + 1% of sales equals $179.

EvaluateTask B: Outsource, Offshore, or Keep It In-House?

Scenario: CentralValley Health Billing, a Stockton company, runs a 40-person customer service call center that answers questions about patients' medical bills. Leadership is considering three options to cut costs. Choose the option you would recommend and defend it using criteria from Lesson 14.3, such as cost, culture and language, time zones, control, and privacy laws.
0 words (aim for 60+)
Expert evaluation: More than one answer is defensible, but a strong case can be made for Option 2.
  • Privacy laws: medical billing involves protected health and financial data. Sending it abroad adds legal risk, since each country has its own data-protection laws.
  • Culture and language: the reading notes a backlash in which some companies moved call centers back to the U.S. (insourcing) so customers could talk with people who share their culture. Billing questions are often stressful, so communication quality matters.
  • Cost: a knowledge base can cut call volume. Citrix reduced calls to its call centers by 30% within the first month.
  • Why not Option 1: lower labor costs and possible 24-hour coverage are real benefits, but they come with privacy, language, and control trade-offs.
  • Why not Option 3: offshoring relocates an entire operation. That's far more disruptive than the cost problem requires, and it's a strategy mostly used for labor-intensive manufacturing.
A strong answer for Option 1 would need to address privacy safeguards and service quality directly.

CreateTask C: Design an Ecommerce Plan for a Local Business

Scenario: The Ramirez family runs a small bakery in Stockton, known for conchas and custom celebration cakes. All their sales happen in the store today. Write a one-page ecommerce plan that takes the bakery online.

Self-assess your plan against this checklist:

0 of 7 criteria met
Model plan (one strong possibility):
  • Type: B2C for online cake orders, plus B2B catering orders from local offices and schools.
  • Hosting: a hosted ecommerce platform such as Shopify on a Basic plan (fewer than 100 SKUs). The host provides the catalog, cart, checkout, and backups with high uptime, which the family couldn't easily maintain themselves. Use a responsive site so phone shoppers can order easily.
  • Marketing: SEO for searches like "custom cakes Stockton," an Instagram and Facebook page showing cake photos, and location-based mobile coupons for people near the store on slow afternoons. Combine these with the shop's in-store signage (integrated marketing).
  • Payments: secure (encrypted) card checkout plus digital wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay. For custom cakes, take a deposit when the order is placed.
  • Delivery: pickup time slots, plus same-day local delivery within a short radius, because baked goods are perishable and national shipping isn't practical.
  • Reputation: ask happy customers for Google and Yelp reviews, and respond politely to every review, including the negative ones.
  • Ethics and privacy: collect only the customer data the bakery needs, post a privacy policy, and let customers opt in to email offers rather than tracking their location without consent.
